Bias & Risk — we all have blind spots. The process helps us catch them.
Structured hiring doesn't eliminate bias — but it makes it visible. Know the patterns and how we counter them.
Watch out for
How we counter it
✕ Similarity bias
Hiring people who remind us of ourselves
✓ Structured questions prepared in advance — same for every candidate
Removes room for improvisation that tends to favour familiar personalities
✕ Halo effect
One strong trait colouring the whole impression
✓ Same case study for every candidate in the same role
Creates a common baseline so evaluation reflects thinking, not one-off performance
✕ Overvaluing charisma or presentation polish
Mistaking confidence and charm for actual competence
✓ Written feedback submitted individually before group discussion
Prevents the loudest or earliest voice from anchoring the group's view
✕ Language fluency bias
Mistaking fluency for intelligence
✓ Evidence-based scoring only — specific examples, not general impressions
Separates what was actually demonstrated from how articulate the candidate sounded
✕ Prestige bias
Brand names and company logos on the CV
✓ Evaluate demonstrated impact — not where they worked, but what they delivered
A well-known employer on a CV says nothing about individual contribution
✕ Speed bias
Strong early impression that locks in a verdict
✓ Reserve final judgment until all steps in the process are complete
Early impressions are hard to override — protect the process from premature conclusions
When to stop a process immediately
✕ Clear competence gap identified
✕ Significant values misalignment
✕ Major expectation misalignment on role, salary, or ways of working
✕ Integrity concerns of any kind

Never continue the process to "see if it improves." Trust your structured evaluation and act on it.

Step 1 · Required for all roles

Startup Meeting & Role Definition

Duration: ~30 min Owner: Hiring Manager + Talent Partner Output: Completed Role Brief / Intake Form
Purpose

Align the Hiring Manager and Talent Partner on the role, define success criteria, agree on the recruitment process, and set expectations for collaboration and communication throughout the recruitment process.

The Startup Meeting is the foundational step in any successful recruitment process. Skipping or rushing this step is one of the most common causes of slow, misaligned, or unsuccessful hiring processes. Investing 30 minutes upfront saves weeks of rework.

Role Definition Framework
Role Context & Business Need
  • Why is this role needed? (New headcount, backfill, restructure, in budget forecast?)
  • What business problem does this hire solve?
  • How does this role contribute to team and company goals?
  • Who will this person work with and report to?
Must-Have vs. Nice-to-Have Criteria
✦Must-Have (Non-Negotiable)
  • Core skills and experience required
  • Minimum years of relevant experience
  • Technical or functional knowledge required
  • Mandatory qualifications or certifications
  • Minimum experience of AI-tools
✧Nice-to-Have (Preferred)
  • Industry or domain experience
  • Exposure to specific tools or platforms
  • Additional languages or international experience
  • Soft skills or leadership qualities
Compensation & Leveling
  • Agreed salary range and total compensation structure
  • Job level and internal grade (where applicable)
  • Flexibility on compensation for exceptional candidates
Work Arrangement
  • Location requirements: on-site, hybrid, or fully remote
  • Office attendance expectations if hybrid
  • Relocation support availability (if applicable)
  • Make sure that everyone involved in the hiring process has added their work calendar to Teamtailor
Recruitment Process & Timeline
Typical Process
Stage 1Talent Partner Screening (30 min) — Culture fit, motivation, career narrative, compensation alignment
Stage 2Hiring Manager Interview (45–60 min) — In-depth competency and role-specific discussion, values and culture
Stage 3Technical or Case Assessment (if applicable) — Skills-based evaluation relevant to the role
Stage 4Reference check (if preferred)
Stage 5Final Decision & Offer — Debrief, decision, and offer stage managed by the Talent Partner
Target Timeline
  • Target start date for the new hire
  • Agreed deadline for the job ad to be unpublished
  • Any critical milestones or blackout periods (e.g., holidays, leadership travel)
Communication Rhythm
  • Preferred channel for updates (ATS should be the default channel, could also be email or Slack)
  • Frequency of progress check-ins
Outcome

Role brief completed. Owner: Hiring Manager + Talent Partner.

✓ GO
Move forward
 Role brief completed, must-haves defined, process agreed. Sourcing and advertising can begin.
✗ STOP
Do not proceed
 Role not approved, budget unclear, or no alignment on requirements. Do not open the role.

Step 2 · Recommended for all roles

Talent Partner Screening

Duration: ~30–35 min Owner: Talent Partner Output: Evaluation notes + recommendation
Purpose

Ensure that criteria regarding experience, motivation, communication, and expectations are met before investing additional time from the Hiring Manager and stakeholders. Create a consistent and fair first evaluation. Provide the candidate with initial clarity about the role and process.

Interview Structure
5 minIntroduction — Set context, introduce the process, and make the candidate comfortable
10 minBackground & Motivation — Explore career history, current work, and drivers for change
10 minRole & Company Match — Assess alignment with the role's responsibilities and environment. Also ask about experience with AI-tools
5 minSalary expectations and start date / notice period
5 minCandidate Questions & Closing — Allow the candidate to ask questions and confirm next steps
Opening Script

"Hi, my name is [Name] and I work as a Talent Partner at [Company]. The purpose of today's conversation is to get to know you better, learn more about your background and motivation, tell you more about the role, and answer any questions you may have."

Core Question Areas
Background
  • Can you briefly walk me through your background and experiences?
  • What products/projects are you currently working with?
  • What kind of environment and technologies are you working in today?
Motivation
  • What made you apply for this role?
  • Why are you considering a change right now?
  • What motivates you in your role?
Role & Company Fit
  • What kind of responsibilities are you most interested in?
  • What type of team or culture do you thrive in?
  • Do you have any experience with AI-tools? (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini etc.)
Practical Alignment
  • What are your salary expectations?
  • What is your notice period or availability?
  • Are you currently in other processes?

Evaluation Areas
1. Basic Experience Match Relevant experience, seniority, ownership
✓ Strong
  • Clear examples of similar responsibilities
  • Demonstrated accountability
  • Level matches role expectations
✗ Weak
  • Significant gap in experience
  • Mostly supportive responsibilities
2. Motivation Why this role, company, now?
✓ Strong
  • Specific and well-researched motivation
  • Alignment between career goals and role scope
  • Interest in impact, growth, ownership
✗ Weak
  • Generic reasoning
  • Focus solely on salary/title
  • Misalignment with what the role offers
3. Communication Clarity, structure, self-awareness
✓ Strong
  • Structured answers (context → action → result)
  • Clear description of personal ownership
  • Reflective and thoughtful
✗ Weak
  • Vague descriptions
  • Difficulty explaining role or impact
  • Avoids specifics
4. Expectations & Practical Alignment Salary, availability, location
✓ Strong
  • Realistic expectations
  • Transparent and clear communication
  • Thoughtful long-term perspective
✗ Weak
  • Large compensation gap
  • Unrealistic growth expectations
  • Vague answers on availability

Step 3 · Required for all roles

Hiring Manager Interview

Duration: ~45–60 min Owner: Hiring Manager Output: Structured evaluation + recommendation to TP
Purpose

Assess role-specific competence in depth, evaluate problem-solving and ownership level, validate seniority alignment, ensure mutual understanding of role expectations and scope, and include a focused check for alignment with core values.

Interview Structure
5 minIntroduction & Context Setting — Frame the conversation and set candidate expectations
20–25 minDeep Dive into Experience — Explore specific projects, decisions, outcomes and learnings
10–15 minRole-Specific Scenarios / Problem Solving — Test applied thinking and judgment
10 minValues Check — A brief set of questions to assess how the candidate operates in practice
5 minExpectations Alignment & Candidate Questions — Align on scope, ownership and development
Opening Script

"Hi, I'm [Name], Hiring Manager for this role. In this conversation, I'd like to better understand your experience in depth, how you approach problems, and how you would contribute to this role. I'll also give you more context around the team, expectations, and current challenges."

Core Question Areas
A. Experience Deep Dive
  • Can you walk me through a project or initiative you are particularly proud of?
  • What was your specific responsibility and what challenges did you face?
  • What was the outcome, and what would you do differently today?
  • Follow-up: personal contribution vs. team, how you measured success, how you prioritized
B. Ownership & Accountability
  • Describe a situation where something did not go as planned
  • Tell me about a time you had to make a difficult decision
  • How do you handle conflicting priorities?
C. Stakeholder & Collaboration
  • What types of stakeholders do you typically work with?
  • How do you manage disagreements?
  • How do you influence without authority?
D. Role-Specific Capability
  • How would you approach [core responsibility of role]?
  • What would your first 90 days look like?
  • How do you ensure quality in your work?
  • For senior roles: How do you work with setting strategy? How do you develop others?
E. Expectations Alignment
  • What level of ownership are you looking for?
  • What type of manager do you perform best with?
  • What are your development expectations in the next 2–3 years?
F. Values Check
  • Describe a mistake you made at work. How did you handle it, and what did you learn?
  • Tell me about a time you went beyond your formal role. What motivated you?
  • Tell me about a time your work created meaningful impact. How did you measure it?

Evaluation Areas
1. Role Competence
✓ Strong
  • Specific examples with measurable impact
  • Clear understanding of complexity
  • Connects experience to this role
✗ Weak
  • Theoretical knowledge only
  • Vague achievements
  • Cannot explain decisions made
2. Ownership & Drive
✓ Strong
  • Initiates beyond scope
  • Owns outcomes including failures
  • Evidence of independent judgment
✗ Weak
  • Reactive, not proactive
  • Deflects responsibility
  • Waits to be told what to do
3. Stakeholder & Collaboration
✓ Strong
  • Navigates complexity with EQ
  • Influences without authority
  • Manages up and sideways
✗ Weak
  • Conflict avoidant
  • Relies on hierarchy
  • Poor awareness of others
4. Values Alignment
✓ Strong
  • Demonstrates accountability through specific evidence
  • Shows initiative and outcome orientation
✗ Weak
  • General statements without examples
  • Avoids talking about failures

Case Study Interview

Duration: ~60 min Owner: Senior team member or stakeholder Output: Structured evaluation + recommendation to TP
Purpose

Assess applied competence in a realistic scenario. Evaluate structured problem-solving, decision-making logic, communication clarity and seniority.

Format & Case Design Principles

The format must be consistent across all candidates applying for the same role. The case may be:

  • Take-home: sent 24–72 hours before interview by the Talent Partner
  • Live: presented during the interview session
Case Design Rules
  • Same case for all candidates applying for the same role
  • Clearly defined objective and realistic (but not confidential) data
  • Appropriate complexity for seniority level
  • Focus on reasoning, not perfection

Avoid: overly academic cases, excessively time-consuming assignments, any case that amounts to free consulting work.

Interview Structure
5 minIntroduction & Framing — Set context and explain the evaluation approach
20–30 minCandidate Presentation — Candidate walks through solution and reasoning
20–25 minDeep Dive Questions — Dig deeper into decisions made, trade-offs considered, assumptions behind them
5 minWrap-up — Close, thank the candidate, and outline next steps
Opening Script

"Today, we'd like to understand how you approach a realistic challenge related to this role. We are not looking for a perfect answer — we're interested in your reasoning, prioritization, and how you structure your thinking. We'll ask follow-up questions to understand your decisions more deeply."

Evaluation Areas
1. Problem Structuring
✓ Strong
  • Clear structure before diving in
  • Identifies key variables and trade-offs
  • Makes assumptions explicit
✗ Weak
  • Jumps straight into solutions
  • No clear framework
  • Disorganized reasoning
2. Analytical & Strategic Thinking
✓ Strong
  • Prioritizes high-impact actions
  • Connects decisions to business outcomes
  • Understands second-order effects
✗ Weak
  • Superficial analysis
  • Ignores constraints
  • No prioritization logic
3. Decision-Making & Judgment
✓ Strong
  • Makes clear decisions with rationale
  • Comfortable with uncertainty
  • Adjusts reasoning when challenged
✗ Weak
  • Avoids committing
  • Defensive under questioning
  • Overcomplicates simple decisions
4. Communication & Stakeholder Thinking
✓ Strong
  • Structured storytelling
  • Clear executive summary
  • Tailors reasoning to audience
✗ Weak
  • Hard to follow
  • No audience awareness
  • Cannot separate key from detail
Seniority Calibration
LevelWhat to look for
JuniorLogical thinking, basic prioritization, structured reasoning
SeniorStrategic framing, trade-off clarity, strong ownership perspective
Lead / ManagerSystems thinking, stakeholder balancing, scalable solutions, team impact consideration
Outcome
1–2
Do not proceed
 Reasoning or communication clearly below the bar for this role.
3
Discuss with TP
 Mixed signals. Discuss before deciding whether to move forward.
4–5
Move forward
 Strong applied thinking. TP schedules Leadership Interview or References.

Step 5 · Senior hires only

Leadership Team Interview

Duration: 60 min Owner: 1–2 Leadership Team members Output: Individual written scorecards per dimension
Purpose

Evaluate strategic maturity, leadership philosophy, and long-term value creation. Assess whether the candidate can think at enterprise level and raise the leadership bar. CEO participation required for executive-level roles.

Interview Structure
5–7 minIntroduction & Framing — Set the strategic tone
12 minStrategic & Client Orientation — Long-term thinking and commercial awareness
12 minTrust-Based Leadership — Psychological safety and accountability at scale
12 minDelivery & Elevation — Reliability, execution, and exceeding expectations
12 minEnterprise Impact & Growth — Organizational influence and long-term partnership
5–7 minReflection & Candidate Questions
Opening Script

"This conversation focuses on how you operate at a strategic and leadership level: how you build trust, create long-term client value, elevate beyond delivery, and shape organizations. We are interested in real examples and your leadership philosophy."

Assessment Framework

Each Leadership Team member owns a specific section. All written feedback must be submitted before group discussion. Differences of more than 1 point should be calibrated.

1. Strategic & Client Orientation Commercial awareness, long-term value
  • How do you understand a client's long-term ambition?
  • Tell us about a time you reshaped a client's direction beyond the initial brief
  • How do you balance short-term delivery with long-term positioning?
✓ Strong
  • Thinks beyond scope
  • Connects work to strategy
  • Speaks in outcomes
✗ Weak
  • Tactical mindset only
  • No commercial perspective
  • Reactive
2. Trust-Based Leadership Psychological safety, integrity
  • How do you create an environment where people challenge you?
  • Tell us about a time you admitted you were wrong
  • How do you handle underperformance while maintaining trust?
✓ Strong
  • Demonstrates vulnerability
  • Invites dissent
  • Consistent ethical behavior
✗ Weak
  • Ego-driven
  • Avoids difficult conversations
  • Authority over influence
3. Deliver Reliably — Then Elevate Execution, opportunity spotting
  • How do you ensure consistent delivery across teams?
  • When have you elevated a relationship beyond expectations?
  • How do you decide when to challenge vs. comply?
✓ Strong
  • Clear delivery systems
  • Builds trust through consistency
  • Brings in right expertise
✗ Weak
  • Over-indexed on innovation without structure
  • Micromanagement
4. Enterprise Impact & Long-Term Partnership Org influence, talent amplification
  • How do you create impact beyond your direct team?
  • How do you develop the next generation of leaders?
  • What's your approach to building long-term partnerships?
✓ Strong
  • Cross-functional influence
  • Develops others deliberately
  • Long-term partnership mindset
✗ Weak
  • Operates in silos
  • No talent development focus

Outcome
Do not proceed
 Strategic thinking or leadership maturity clearly below bar. Stop the process.
Proceed with caution
 Concerns exist. Calibrate scores and align with HM before continuing.
Strong proceed
 Clear leadership signal. TP moves to References and Offer preparation.

Step 6 · Recommended before offer

References

Duration: 15–25 min per referee Owner: Talent Partner Output: Written reference summary per referee
Purpose

Validate key signals from the process, verify factual claims, and surface any risks or concerns before extending an offer. Reference checks are a structured verification step — not a formality.

Reference Selection
  • Minimum two references; ideally three
  • At least one must be a direct line manager
  • References should be recent (within the last 3–5 years)
  • Avoid character references or personal contacts only
  • The candidate provides referee details. The Talent Partner contacts referees directly and schedules calls.
Call Structure
5 minIntroduction — Set context, explain purpose and confirm the referee's relationship to the candidate
15 minCore questions — Cover performance, collaboration, ownership and growth areas
5 minClosing — Rehire question and any final observations
Opening Script

"Hi, my name is [Name] and I work as a Talent Partner at [Company]. We are in the final stages of our recruitment process with [Candidate Name] for a [Role] position. This is a structured reference — I have a few specific questions about their performance and working style. Everything shared will be treated confidentially within the hiring team."

Core Questions
Role & Performance
  • In what capacity and for how long did you work with [Candidate]?
  • What were their primary responsibilities?
  • What were their strongest contributions?
  • In what areas did you see the most room for growth?
Collaboration & Working Style
  • How did [Candidate] work with peers, stakeholders and leadership?
  • How did they handle pressure, uncertainty or tight deadlines?
  • Can you describe a challenging situation they navigated?
Leadership & Ownership
  • How did [Candidate] take ownership of their work?
  • Did they show initiative beyond what was explicitly asked?
  • How did they respond to feedback and critique?
Closing & Rehire
  • Would you rehire [Candidate] given the opportunity? Why or why not?
  • Is there anything else we should be aware of as we make our decision?
Evaluation Dimensions
1. Performance Consistency

Did strengths described match what was observed in the process? Is the track record consistent?

2. Collaboration & Culture Fit

How did they operate within teams? Any signs of conflict or trust issues?

3. Leadership & Ownership

Did they take initiative? Were they accountable for outcomes, including setbacks?

4. Rehire Indicator

A clear yes from a direct manager is a strong signal. A conditional yes or no requires documentation and discussion with the Hiring Manager.

Outcome
1–2
Do not proceed
 Concerns confirmed or new risks surfaced. Discuss with HM before any further steps.
3
Discuss
 Mixed or qualified responses. Calibrate with Hiring Manager before extending offer.
4–5
Move forward to offer
 Positive references. Process confirmed. Proceed to Decision & Offer.

Step 7 · Required for all roles

Decision & Offer

Duration: Calibration 30–45 min Owner: Hiring Manager + Talent Partner Output: Documented decision, formal offer letter, confirmed start date
Purpose

Align the hiring team on a clear, evidence-based decision, prepare a competitive and fair offer, and manage the offer conversation and any negotiations professionally.

Calibration Meeting Structure

Before any offer is extended, all interviewers should submit written scorecards independently.

10 minScorecard review — Each interviewer presents their scores and key observations
15–20 minEvidence discussion — Discuss each evaluation dimension with specific examples
10 minFinal decision — Agree on hire / no hire and offer level
⚠️

Avoid group discussion before all written feedback has been submitted.

Preparing the Offer

Once a decision to proceed has been made, agree internally before contacting the candidate:

  • Salary, bonus structure and total compensation package
  • Offer level and seniority title
  • Start date range based on the candidate's notice period
  • What is negotiable — and what is not — before the call
⚠️

Do not contact the candidate until internal alignment is complete and the written offer letter is ready to issue.

The Offer Conversation

"Hi [Name], I have some great news — we have been very impressed throughout the process, and we would love to offer you the [Role] position at [Company]. I'd like to walk you through the offer and give you time to ask any questions."

5 minOffer headline — Role, title, team, and anticipated start date
10 minCompensation details — Salary, bonus, benefits and any equity or other elements
10 minCandidate questions & initial reaction — Listen actively; do not oversell
5 minNext steps — Confirm timeline for their decision and when the written offer will be sent
Handling Negotiations

It is normal for candidates to negotiate. The Talent Partner leads the conversation.

  • Never make commitments without prior alignment with the Hiring Manager and Finance
  • Give the candidate 24–72 hours to consider — avoid applying pressure
  • Document everything agreed verbally before issuing any revised letter
  • If a candidate declines, gather feedback and share with the Hiring Manager
Common Negotiation Topics
TopicGuidance
SalaryBe clear on range and what drives flexibility. A competing offer requires documented evidence.
Start dateCheck notice period constraints. Agree on the latest acceptable start date with the HM.
TitleEscalate to the Hiring Manager if not resolved upfront. Do not improvise.
Other benefitsTP and HM must align before responding to any non-standard requests.
Decision Framework (4 Checks Before Offer)
1. Overall Fit

Does the candidate meet must-have criteria? Is the team aligned on competence, values and seniority level?

2. Offer Level Alignment

Is the proposed compensation fair, competitive and within the agreed budget? Has Finance approved?

3. Negotiation Outcome

Have all negotiated items been agreed internally and documented? Is the revised offer ready to issue?

4. Start Date Confirmed

Has the candidate confirmed a start date? Is preboarding initiated? Owner: Talent Partner.

Outcome
👍 Accepted
 Offer accepted and start date confirmed. Initiate preboarding. Close role in Teamtailor.
👎 Declined
 Candidate rejects offer. Gather feedback. Discuss with HM whether to proceed with other candidates or re-open the search.
